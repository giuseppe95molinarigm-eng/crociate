## General notes (apply to all three volumes)

- **Series name in KDP:** set the *Series* field to **The Crusades** (or **The Crusades Trilogy**) and number the volumes 1, 2 and 3. Amazon then links the three books on one series page, and each listing shows "Book 1 of 3". That's free cross-selling between the volumes.
- **Description length:** KDP allows up to 4,000 characters. Each description below is about 1,600–2,000 characters. That's long enough to rank and short enough that people read it. The first ~200 characters show above the "Read more" fold, so the opening hook matters most.
- **Formatting:** KDP's description editor supports bold, italics and bulleted lists. Each volume has a plain-text version and an HTML version. Paste the HTML version if you use the HTML / source mode, or format the plain version with the editor buttons.
- **Backend keyword rules:** KDP gives you 7 boxes of up to 50 characters each. Words that are already in your title, subtitle or author name are indexed anyway, so the keywords below avoid repeating them. The boxes are for *extra* search terms. Don't use other authors' names, other book titles, or claims like "bestseller" or "free". Amazon forbids them and can suppress the listing.
- **Categories:** KDP now lets you pick **3 categories** per format during setup. Each volume has 3 primary picks and 2 alternates. Category names in the KDP picker change from time to time, so pick the closest match if a label differs slightly. Check paperback and Kindle separately, because the two have separate category trees.

# VOLUME I: *The Crusades: Origin, Society and Politics*

## 1. Amazon Book Description

### Plain text

**Why did tens of thousands of men and women leave everything behind to march toward Jerusalem? The answer is far more complex than faith alone.**

The first volume of David R. Bardi's Crusades trilogy goes back to the roots of the most controversial movement of the Middle Ages. Before the sieges and the battles came ideas, ambitions and fears. Bardi traces the religious, political and social forces that gave birth to crusading ideology, from the Latin West to the Byzantine Empire and the Islamic world.

The book moves beyond the traditional "holy war" narrative. It reveals a medieval world that was connected, contested and surprisingly modern in its tensions. Popes pursued authority, emperors sought allies, and kings and regional lords chased power and prestige. Preachers and propagandists turned a call to arms into a mass movement. Ordinary people joined, supported, resisted or simply suffered the consequences.

**In this volume you will discover:**

- The religious, political and social origins of the Crusades
- The key figures who shaped the movement: popes, emperors, kings and regional rulers
- The societies of the Latin West, the Byzantine Empire and the Islamic world on the eve of conflict
- How ideology, preaching and propaganda mobilized an entire continent
- The complex motivations of those who joined, supported or opposed the Crusades
- The cultural, political and diplomatic context of the 11th and 12th centuries

Written by a historian of the medieval Mediterranean, this clear and well-researched account is ideal for history enthusiasts, students and anyone who wants to understand how the encounter between Christianity and Islam shaped the medieval world, and why it still matters today.

**This is Book 1 of a three-volume series.** Continue with *Volume II: Economy in Motion, Logistics, Finance and Trade* and *Volume III: Collision, Integration and Institutions*.

# VOLUME II: *The Crusades: Economy in Motion, Logistics, Finance and Trade*

## 1. Amazon Book Description

### Plain text

**Wars are won with money, ships and supplies long before they are fought. The Crusades were no exception.**

Behind every army that marched east stood a vast and largely unseen machine. Grain had to be bought, horses shipped and fleets chartered. Loans were raised, taxes levied and routes secured across land and sea. In the second volume of his Crusades trilogy, historian David R. Bardi steps off the battlefield to follow the money, the goods and the people that made the crusading movement possible.

This is the economic story of the Crusades. Bardi shows how men, capital, merchandise and ideas circulated between Europe, the Mediterranean and the Near East. He shows how the Italian maritime republics built commercial empires on the back of holy war. And he shows how the encounter between Latin Christendom and the Islamic world sparked lasting changes in commerce, finance, technology and institutions.

**In this volume you will discover:**

- The material and financial resources required to launch and sustain the Crusades
- How supply, transport and communication were organized across land and sea
- The role of Venice, Genoa, Pisa and the Mediterranean trade networks
- The movement of people, goods and knowledge between East and West
- The economic impact of the Crusades on European and Near Eastern societies
- The legacy of crusading exchange in the medieval and early modern world

For readers of military history, economic history and medieval trade, this book offers a fresh view of a familiar subject. It explains how the business of crusading helped lay the foundations of the modern economy.

**This is Book 2 of a three-volume series.** Begin with *Volume I: Origin, Society and Politics* and conclude with *Volume III: Collision, Integration and Institutions*.

# VOLUME III: *The Crusades: Collision, Integration and Institutions*

## 1. Amazon Book Description

### Plain text

**The Crusades did not end when the last fortress fell. Their true legacy was only beginning.**

For two centuries, Latin Christians, Byzantines and Muslims fought, traded, negotiated and lived side by side across the eastern Mediterranean. What emerged from that long encounter was more than a story of conquest and defeat. New states, new institutions and new ways of governing, judging and living together reshaped Europe, the Mediterranean and the Near East.

In the final volume of his Crusades trilogy, historian David R. Bardi looks past the battlefield to the deeper processes of conflict, coexistence and integration. He covers the founding of the Crusader states and the rise of the military orders. He also traces the development of law, administration and urban institutions. Bardi reveals how the Crusades left a complex and lasting legacy of interaction and adaptation that reached into the early modern world.

**In this volume you will discover:**

- The establishment and evolution of the Crusader states in the Levant
- The Templars, Hospitallers and other military orders, and their institutional and economic influence
- The processes of coexistence, exchange and cultural integration between faiths
- The development of legal, administrative and urban institutions
- The impact of the Crusades on European, Byzantine and Islamic societies
- The long-term legacy of the Crusades in the medieval and early modern world

Thoughtful, balanced and accessible, this book is essential reading for anyone interested in medieval history, the relationship between Christianity and Islam, and the long roots of the world we live in today.

**This is Book 3 of a three-volume series.** Read it alongside *Volume I: Origin, Society and Politics* and *Volume II: Economy in Motion, Logistics, Finance and Trade* for the complete picture.

# Title & Subtitle Alternatives

**Strategy:** "The Crusades" is the strongest search term in the niche, so every option keeps it at the front of the title. Each subtitle starts with the highest-traffic phrase readers type (for example "Medieval Economy", "Crusader States" or "Holy War"). It then adds a clear promise. A volume number makes the series order obvious in search results.

> **Important KDP note:** once a paperback is **published**, KDP won't let you change its title or subtitle. A new title needs a new edition with a new ISBN, plus a new cover, because the cover text must match the metadata exactly. If the books are still in draft or review, you can change titles freely. The **current titles also work fine** with the descriptions and keywords above.
